## High priority

- **Next.js major-version upgrade (14 → 16) — deliberately deferred out of the Aug 2026 dependency-audit round.** `npm audit` currently flags 2 high-severity issues (`next`, `postcss`) that only resolve via `next@16.3.3`; npm itself calls this "a breaking change," and one bundled advisory (`GHSA-9g9p-9gw9-jx7f`, a DoS via Image Optimizer `remotePatterns`) specifically matches this project's own wide-open `images.remotePatterns: [{ hostname: '**' }]` config. Everything else from that audit shipped this round (see `CHANGELOG.md`) — this is the one piece that needs its own dedicated round: upgrade on a branch, run the full test suite and a real `next build`, then hand-check every page/API route rather than trusting a two-major-version jump blind on a live site.

**Visual identity tailored to what someone actually collects — fully shipped** (all four pieces documented in `CHANGELOG.md`: the segmented-shelf hero and its color/icon system, type-specific Insights widgets, the same identity extended to public profiles, and type-aware microcopy + trophy flavor text). No longer tracked here — see `CHANGELOG.md` for the full build history of this initiative if you want to revisit any piece of it.

**Mobile layout feels disorganized in places — audit complete, one bug found and fixed.** Two rounds of a live-browser mobile audit (375×812 viewport) covered the home page, leaderboard, three public profiles (game-heavy, comic-heavy, and a Sonic/retro-heavy one, to check the shelf hero and multi-type collection grid under different dominant types), `/lists`, `/whats-new`, `/accessibility`, `/feedback`, `/signup`, `/login`, `/collectible-database`, `/players`, and — closing the gap the first round couldn't reach — the client-side Trophies/Comments tab toggle on a profile and an item-detail series modal (opened by tapping a collection tile), both of which needed real click interaction rather than just URL navigation. Found and fixed one concrete, confirmed bug: the site footer had no `flex-wrap`, so its last link overflowed past the viewport edge and was genuinely unreachable; see `CHANGELOG.md`. Every other surface checked, across both rounds, rendered cleanly with no overflow or unreachable content. No longer tracked as an open item — a future report of something specific on mobile is a fresh bug, not a continuation of this one.
- **Widened `games` RLS policy for `wishlist_public` technically permits wishlist rows through *any* games query, not just the gift list page** — noticed while building it (see `CHANGELOG.md`): the policy change is scoped to `ownership = 'wishlist'` rows only, and both existing views that query another user's `games` (`/compare/[username]`, the shelf mosaic) were checked and are safe — both already gate on `is_public` at the app level before ever running the query, so nothing leaks today. But any *future* feature that queries `games` for a non-owner without an explicit `is_public` check would now silently see that profile's wishlist items even when the profile is otherwise private. Worth keeping in mind rather than a build item on its own — flagging so it isn't a surprise later.
- **Worth watching: crowdsourced series matching now fetches every row of the item type, not just matches** — noticed while adding normalization to `lib/seriesCrowdsource.js` (see `CHANGELOG.md`): merging near-duplicate series names (punctuation/spacing differences) properly means comparing normalized text in JS, which can't be done as a server-side exact match — so it now pulls every row of that `item_type` and filters client-side instead of letting Postgres narrow it down first. Fine at today's scale (Funko Pops only, RLS-scoped to public profiles plus the viewer's own), but would need a real search index if this ever handled a type with meaningfully more rows.

## Medium priority

- **Full physical-release catalogue per console** — pick a platform (e.g. "PlayStation 2"), see every game ever released for it, greyed out except what's logged. IGDB has the per-platform list, but it's thousands of entries for any major system, so this needs real pagination/search within the view itself.
- **Magic master sets** — the Pokémon side of this shipped (see `CHANGELOG.md`: "See master set," a real per-set, per-variant TCGdex checklist replacing the old crowdsourced trading-card series view). Magic falls back to the crowdsourced-from-Shelf-Life's-own-data version now (see `CHANGELOG.md` — it was actually silently routed to the Pokémon-only TCGdex endpoint and getting nothing at all until that fix). Scryfall has clean, free per-set card data (already used for Magic search) including variant/frame info, so the same approach TCGdex got should carry over as a real Magic-specific backend — just needs its own pass since Scryfall's data shapes (and what counts as a "variant" — foil, borderless, showcase, etc.) aren't identical to TCGdex's.
- **Pokémon master sets: show variants you don't own yet, not just confirm the ones you do** — the shipped version only expands a card into its real Normal/Reverse/Holo/1st Edition tiles once you've already logged a variant copy of it (deliberate — fetching real variant data for every card in a 100-250+ card set on every click risks a timeout and hammers TCGdex's free API). A precomputed/cached version of each set's full variant breakdown (refreshed periodically rather than fetched live per click) would close the gap and let the grid show "you're missing the reverse holo of this" for cards you've never touched, which is closer to what a completionist actually wants from a master-set tracker.
- **Extend "See full series" to every remaining collectible type** — currently games, comics, trading cards, and Funko Pops only; Vinyl, CDs, DVDs, VHS, Books, and Consoles have none. No natural numbered-series field for these, so "series" needs its own per-type definition (likely grouping by artist/director/author).
- **Rest of the Accessibility checklist** — down to one item: a manual text-size control, blocked on a real technical obstacle. `app/globals.css` has hundreds of scattered hardcoded `font-size: NNpx` declarations with no root rem/em scale feeding them, so a root-font-size-based control wouldn't cascade to most of the site's text; CSS `zoom` would work but shouldn't ship without visually verifying it first, on a real dev server. Everything else in the original list is now closed (see `CHANGELOG.md` for each): contrast audit (WCAG AA — fixed a real light-mode-only bug in `--owned`/`--sold`/`--tier-bronze`/`--tier-silver`/`--tier-gold`), differentiating status without color alone (podium and trophy tiers both already render the status as text, no fix needed), confirming pinch-to-zoom isn't disabled (already fine), the full keyboard tab-order check across the dashboard grid/leaderboard/search (found and fixed one real bug — the leaderboard podium's Tab order didn't match its left-to-right visual layout; dashboard grid and search were already clean), keyboard support for the star rating, `prefers-reduced-motion` site-wide, a pausable Undo-delete countdown, and a public `/accessibility` page. See "Accessibility (full list)" below for the complete, organized version.
- **Image handling for cover art — half closed.** The "at least validate/gracefully degrade" half is done (see `CHANGELOG.md`): every cover-rendering spot site-wide now falls back to a placeholder instead of the browser's default broken-image icon when a URL is dead. Still open: actually downloading and re-hosting cover art in Supabase Storage instead of trusting external URLs long-term — a real infra/cost decision (storage scales with usage), not a quick follow-up.
- **Population data for graded items** — trading cards/comics already have a Grade field; for a graded item, pulling the population count for that specific grade from PSA's or CGC's public lookup tools (where available) would show how rare that exact grade actually is, right alongside the grade itself.
- **Chat-style quick add** ("logged a Chrono Quest for $40 today") — parse a short typed sentence into a pre-filled Add form instead of picking fields one at a time, for fast bulk logging right after a store run. Speculative, same caveat as AI shelf-photo import below — needs real testing on parse accuracy before promising it works.

**The item below (plus two more, in "Later" further down) came out of an actual competitor pass (Aug 2026) — Discogs, CLZ/Collectorz, iCollect Everything, Collectr, TCGplayer, League of Comic Geeks, Backloggd, and Grouvee. Full comparison shared in chat; short version: nobody else covers this much breadth *and* real social features *and* real per-type completion data at once, but a few of them each have one specific thing worth borrowing. Two of the original three shipped (see `CHANGELOG.md`: the `/lists` directory closes the Backloggd/Grouvee "browsable community lists" gap, and aggregate ratings now show on both the collectible detail page and search/browse tiles, closing the Backloggd crowd-rating gap — the earlier note claiming the detail page had nothing was actually stale, it's had an avg rating stat since the page itself shipped, the browse-tile visibility was the real remaining gap).**

- **Single-item AI photo auto-fill** — iCollect Everything's standout feature, and a much smaller, more provably-accurate slice of the "AI shelf-photo bulk import" idea already below: point the camera at one item, get title/cover/details auto-filled, instead of a whole shelf at once. Worth doing this first and learning from real accuracy before attempting the harder multi-item version. **Checked what this actually needs to build (Aug 2026):** the camera-capture and Supabase Storage upload plumbing already exists, reusable from `GameModal.jsx`'s condition-photo upload — but there's no vision/image-recognition API configured anywhere in this project today — no Anthropic/OpenAI/Google Vision key, nothing in `.env.local.example`. This is genuinely blocked on you adding a new AI vision API key (and accepting its per-call cost) before it's buildable, not something with a free/already-configured path the way the rest of this tier has been. (Note: barcode scanning, mentioned here in earlier drafts of this note as a source of reusable camera-access code, was removed — see `CHANGELOG.md` — since per-type title search covers auto-fill with better coverage than a UPC lookup ever had; any future camera-capture work here would be built fresh, not adapted from that.)

## Later (bigger, more design work)

- **Shared/household collections** — let a second account co-manage one collection. Real permission/ownership questions to work through, not a quick add.
- **Multi-language support (i18n)** — everything's English-only, fine for a friends-and-family project but a real ceiling for an actual App Store product. Worth it once there's a specific market to justify it.
- **AI shelf-photo bulk import** — one photo of a physical shelf, detect and suggest multiple items at once instead of searching or typing each one in by hand. The most speculative idea here — accuracy on a photo full of spines needs real testing before promising it works.
- **Import from Goodreads / Discogs** — same idea as the existing Steam import, for Books and Vinyl/CDs respectively.
- **Per-item reviews (separate from personal rating)** — a review system letting other users read what people think of a specific item, aggregated site-wide.
- **Wantlist matching / trading** — surface when someone you follow has something on your wishlist, or a duplicate they might trade.
- **Notification digest emails** — an opt-in weekly summary of your own stats or followed collectors' activity.
- **Live currency conversion** — currency is display-only right now; real conversion needs a rates API (the leaderboard-ranking one already added is a start) and a decision on the "true" underlying value across currencies.
- **Xbox / PlayStation account integration** — Steam is done. Xbox is gated behind app registration; PlayStation has no official public API at all (reverse-engineered access only, fragile and ToS-gray-area).
- **Global chat** — a site-wide message board, separate from per-profile comment walls. Needs real-time updates and real moderation once it's not just friends talking.
- **More Shelf Life milestone variety** — platform-completionist badges, genre/decade-spanning badges, room for oddball community-suggested ones beyond "own N items."
- **Even more collectible types** — board games, action figures/toys, coins, whenever there's demand.
- **iPad-specific polish** — the app went in as a Universal build (phone layout scaled up); a real iPad layout would be a nice follow-up if iPad usage turns out meaningful.
- **Native ad revenue from the app itself (AdMob)** — even with AdSense on for the website, the app earns $0 on its own; real native integration (AdMob SDK or Google's WebView Ads API) needs genuine Swift/native work. Not worth it at current traffic.
- **Public read-only API / personal access tokens** — let a collector generate an API key scoped to just their own data, so anyone who wants their collection in a spreadsheet, a personal dashboard, or a script can pull it directly instead of exporting CSVs by hand. Real design work either way: token issuance/revocation UI, rate limiting, and deciding what's actually worth exposing (probably a read-only mirror of what's already visible via the existing RLS rules, nothing new).
- **In-app marketplace: sell to another collector directly, not just an affiliate redirect** — distinct from the affiliate "buy it" links and the "For sale" flag, both already shipped (see `CHANGELOG.md`): those point outward or just show interest, this would be an actual listing another collector could act on inside Shelf Life. Real trust, payment, and dispute questions come with that — easily the biggest-scope item on this list. Worth starting with "list for sale + a way to message the seller" long before anything resembling in-app payments.
- **Native home-screen widgets (iOS/Android)** — today's stats, Play Next, or trophy progress right on the home screen, now that the app is wrapped for both platforms. Needs genuine native work (WidgetKit on iOS, App Widgets on Android) — same caveat as AdMob above, not buildable from this codebase alone.
- **Browser extension: add an item while shopping** — a small "Add to Shelf Life" extension that captures a title/price/image straight off an eBay/Amazon/retailer listing page instead of typing it in from memory later. A genuinely new platform surface — its own codebase, its own store listing and review process (Chrome Web Store this time, not Apple's or Google's).
- **Printable QR/barcode labels per item** — a small sticker linking straight back to an item's Shelf Life entry, for anyone who actually boxes up and stores a physical collection and wants a fast way to look a boxed item back up. Needs real label-sheet layout work (Avery-style templates) to be genuinely useful rather than a novelty.
- **Community-verified master sets** — master-set data currently comes entirely from TCGdex/Comic Vine/IGDB, or Shelf Life's own crowdsourced logs; a "flag this as wrong" or trusted-contributor correction layer would start to matter once master sets cover enough collectible types that data errors actually show up. Same flavor of real moderation/trust question as Global chat above.
- **Pull list / upcoming-release calendar with spend forecasting** — from the Aug 2026 competitor pass (see note above): League of Comic Geeks' single most-loved feature is exactly this — a calendar of what's coming for the things you already track, plus a running "here's what you'll owe this week/month" total. IGDB (games) and Comic Vine (comics) both already expose future release dates to this codebase for other features, so the data itself is reachable; the real work is a calendar UI and getting the spend-forecast math right across two very different collectible types at once.
- **Real sold-price data, not just current listings** — same competitor pass: Collectr and TCGplayer both lean on actual transaction/sold-price data as their credibility anchor for "what's this actually worth." Shelf Life's own eBay price check is explicitly current active-listing prices, not sold comps (a known, already-documented limitation — see `CHANGELOG.md`), because eBay stopped offering free access to sold-listing data. Flagging honestly rather than pretending it's a clean build: closing this gap for real needs either a paid market-data source or something against eBay's ToS, so it's more of an "if the budget ever supports it" item than a normal build.

## Paid / premium ideas (business decisions, not just builds)

Everything Shelf Life does today is free, and there's no payment processor wired up anywhere in this codebase — before any single idea below is buildable, that has to exist first: most likely Stripe given the Next.js/Vercel/Supabase stack, plus a `subscriptions` table, webhook handling, and an entitlements check gating whichever features end up behind it. That's real work on its own, on top of the actual pricing/tier design, which isn't something I can decide — these are flagged here as ideas worth having on file, not prioritized or sequenced like the tiers above.

- **A Pro subscription tier** — the prerequisite for everything else in this section (payment processor + entitlements, described above). Worth deciding what actually goes behind it before building any of it — probably some mix of the ideas below rather than one single "upgrade" button.
- **Real sold-price data** — already flagged in the "Later" tier above as a known gap (Shelf Life's eBay price check is current listings only, since eBay stopped offering free access to sold-listing data). A paid collectibles pricing API (PriceCharting, CardLadder, or similar) could close it for real, but the ongoing per-lookup cost only pencils out behind a Pro tier or a pass-through charge.
- **PSA/CGC population data for graded items** — already a Medium-priority idea (trading cards/comics already have a Grade field; showing how rare that exact grade is would be a nice addition). The reliable, complete population-lookup data on PSA's/CGC's own side tends to sit behind their own paid tiers, so this is really the same "needs a paid upstream data source" shape as sold-price data above.
- **AI photo/shelf auto-fill** — already flagged elsewhere in this file as blocked without a new AI vision API key (nothing like that is configured today, and it'd carry a real per-call cost). A natural Pro-tier perk once billing exists, rather than something offered free to every account.
- **Collection appraisal/insurance PDF export** — the free version has already shipped (see `CHANGELOG.md`: a print-optimized `/dashboard/appraisal` page). A paid tier could go further — an actual generated PDF file (not just print-to-PDF) with a real letterhead/signature block, or bundling in condition photos per item — rather than gating the basic export itself.
- **Boosted/featured "For sale" listings** — the "For sale" flag itself has shipped (see `CHANGELOG.md`); once the in-app marketplace (Later tier) also ships, a "boost this listing" placement — similar spirit to eBay's promoted listings — would be a natural, low-effort monetization hook layered on top of features that are already worth building for their own sake.
- **Bigger photo/storage limits** — condition photos are capped at 4 per item today; Supabase Storage cost scales with actual usage, so a Pro tier raising that cap (more condition photos, higher-res uploads) ties a real infra cost directly to a real, easy-to-understand perk.
- **Ad-free browsing for logged-in users** — AdSense on the website is already planned but not yet live (see the "Needs a quick action from you" section above). Once it is, removing ads for paying accounts is a standard, low-build-effort Pro perk to pair with it.
- **A vanity profile handle or "verified collector" badge** — purely cosmetic, no ongoing cost once billing exists, and the safest first thing to ship behind a Pro tier before committing to any of the pricier, ongoing-cost ideas above.
- **Public read-only API access** — already a "Later"-tier idea (personal access tokens for pulling your own collection into a spreadsheet, dashboard, or script). Token issuance and rate limiting are real ongoing infra and a real support surface, so gating unlimited access behind Pro (rather than opening it to every free account) is worth deciding rather than assuming.
